Bilawal stirs up 'political hornet’s nest,' calls for political reset and end to ‘revenge politics’

PPP Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari calls for a political reset to restore public trust in parliament and state institutions, demands an end to revenge politics, backs returning PTI’s reserved seats and symbol, rejects a referendum on new provinces and warns against any forced division of Sindh

KARACHI: Amid a raging debate over a possible system reset and the creation of new provinces, Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari on Tuesday declared that the governance system had not collapsed on its own but had been “made to collapse”, calling for a political reset to correct “injustices” and restore public confidence in state institutions and parliament.

Speaking at the Karachi Press Club, Bilawal said the remedy on offer did not address the cause of the breakdown, noting that few disputed the collapse but nobody was debating why it happened.

He said fixing the root causes was key to easing poverty, unemployment and inflation, and that the reset had to be political, as politicians represent the people.

Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi had earlier sparked a political storm by proposing new administrative units, saying the current governance system had “collapsed” and needed a “reset”.

He said state institutions had “lost their legitimacy” and that public trust in them had to be rebuilt.

He vowed to resist any “unconstitutional” move on new provinces, opposed a referendum, and urged that the opposition PTI get its reserved seats back as a “confidence-building measure”. He also questioned the judiciary’s state after the 27th Amendment, which he had backed a year ago.

‘Injustices’ to PTI over reserved seats, symbol’

Bilawal said righting certain wrongs could quickly restore the opposition’s confidence, recalling that voters punished the 1988 revocation of the PPP’s own election symbol.

Breaking with the coalition, he called the reserved seats episode a joke, saying treasury members pocketed seats denied to the opposition. The Supreme Court had held the PTI eligible for them after the 2024 polls, but a constitutional bench formed under the 26th Amendment later reversed that.

He noted two amendments had passed on the strength of those seats, with a third mooted, and urged returning them and the PTI’s symbol unconditionally, though the PPP itself gained from the case.

‘Imran Khan a political prisoner’

Bilawal said retaliatory actions had to stop and the culture of revenge in politics end for good if parliament was to function.

He said no one should remain a political prisoner, remarking that since 1947 prime ministers had kept moving between jail and office.

Asked directly, he said he regarded PTI founder Imran Khan as a political prisoner, while acknowledging the PTI’s mistakes and calling for consensus so no future premier is jailed.

‘Federal govt must decide on reset’

Bilawal asked the federal government to state its position on the reset, saying he would build consensus in his party first and did not know the government’s thinking.

Responding to the interior minister’s “same page” remark, he said the debate had been forced without even cabinet discussion and hoped Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif controlled his ministers.

Defence Minister Khawaja Asif has also floated up to 12 provinces. Bilawal said the PPP welcomed debate but saw proposals from outside parliament as a backdoor route.

On any 18th Amendment rollback, he said Sindh gave the Centre a Rs585 billion surplus and Rs260 billion for security, plus about Rs1.7 trillion via the petroleum levy, so the Centre should answer for weak public spending.

‘Referendum has never been democratic’

Bilawal said referendums in Pakistan had always served undemocratic ends and, since the Constitution already provides for new units, the proposal signalled an unconstitutional route the PPP would oppose.

He said the PPP backed new provinces but would resist any forced division of Sindh, warning of bloodshed and citing Sindh Assembly resolutions against it.

He added that the PML-N-led government had not discussed the issue with the PPP.

‘Pressure on PPP lawmakers’

Bilawal said decisions should reflect the people’s wishes and criticised those floating the proposals as people who had never won an election.

He said PPP MNAs and MPAs faced pressure but answered to the public, and would revolt if he ever told them to back Sindh’s division.

He also said the PPP would back nothing on Punjab without its assembly’s approval, and noted little debate on Gilgit-Baltistan’s provincial status.

‘27th Amendment passed with brute majority’

Bilawal said the PPP’s goal of a constitutional court had been met, but questioned public confidence in the new setup, adding that he wanted broader consensus rather than a reversal.

He said the 26th Amendment carried opposition input via Maulana Fazlur Rehman while the 27th relied on a “brute majority”, and noted that rigging claims were now coming from inside the government.

‘JUI-F chief has right to criticise govt’

Bilawal defended JUI-F chief Fazl, who has clashed with federal ministers over provincial rights and governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

He said criticism was the opposition’s right and its role the “most patriotic” one.

Reiterating that a reset must begin in parliament, he said the opposition had real reasons to have lost confidence.

He alleged selective accountability against critics, citing NAB action and a CDA notice on Fazl’s residence, and said governor’s rule in KP could end if the PTI joined the fight against terrorism.
