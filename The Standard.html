South Korea says China trade agreement to boost tourism, entertainment sectors

South Korea and China have substantially concluded follow-up negotiations on services and investment under a bilateral free trade agreement, in a move that will boost the entertainment and tourism sectors,South Korea said on Wednesday.

The agreement is expected to increase bilateral trade in services and investment, currently worth around $38.8 billion, according to a joint South Korean ministerial statement distributed by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism.

It followed talks between Presidents Lee Jae Myung and Xi Jinping at the APEC summit in Shenzhen; Industry Minister Kim Jung-kwan said Korean firms gain a stable footing in China.

For the first time, Beijing will grant Korean movies and games most-favoured nation treatment, assess Korean content without discrimination, co-produce TV shows and open 23 regions to Korean travel agencies.

This may ease curbs dating from a 2016 THAAD missile dispute, which Beijing never formally tied to them. Chinese visitors, nearly 29% of 2025 arrivals, spent $1,504 each on average, 13% above other foreigners.
