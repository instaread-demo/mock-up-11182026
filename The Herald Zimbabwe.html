Dawn of a new era

CABINET has approved the Zimbabwe National Artificial Intelligence Charter, establishing ethical guidelines for the development and deployment of artificial intelligence, while providing for oversight, accountability and mechanisms for handling complaints.

This comes as the country moves to implement its National Artificial Intelligence Strategy (2026–2030), which President Mnangagwa launched in March, describing AI as a critical tool for modernisation, industrialisation and sustainable economic growth.

Ministers also backed updating the arts council law for a creative sector that now spans digital work.

At a Harare briefing, Information Minister Dr Zhemu Soda said the Charter gives the strategy binding ethical commitments to keep AI “human-centric” and constitutional.

Launching the strategy in March, President Mnangagwa placed AI at the centre of a global technological shift.

He urged investment in digital networks so the nation becomes “digitally robust and agile”, citing gains for farming, mining, health, finance, education and communities.

Dr Soda called the Charter a shared pact among Government, business, academia, civil society and citizens, covering AI from research to use, with oversight, complaints channels and Ubuntu/Unhu values.

Finance Minister Professor Mthuli Ncube tabled it as chair of the Cabinet Committee on National Development.

Cabinet also approved principles of the National Arts Council of Zimbabwe Amendment Bill, 2026, from Minister Anselem Sanyatwe. It would modernise a 1985 framework for digital media, streaming, gaming, film and fashion, and strengthen governance and intellectual property protection.
