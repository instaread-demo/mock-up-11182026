Russell Brand’s Wife At Centre Of Speculation As Petition Demanding His Release Hits 20,000 Signatures

A petition supporting convicted rapist Russell Brand has attracted more than 15,000 signatures after being shared on his social media account, prompting speculation about who is behind the campaign while the former comedian serves a lengthy prison sentence.

The online appeal, titled “Justice for Russell Brand”, was promoted through Brand’s X account on Thursday, November 12, just three days after he was sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison for rape and sexual assault.

The post reached an account with roughly 10 million followers, and GB News reported the total had passed 15,000 by the morning of Friday, November 13, drawing criticism from users who objected to backing a man so recently convicted of serious sexual offences.

It is also unclear who controls Brand’s account during his imprisonment. Some online commentators have suggested his wife, Laura Gallacher, may have been involved, but there is no verified evidence that she created, organised or promoted the campaign.

The petition is credited to someone using the name “Jane Doe”, not to Gallacher or any other identified person, and it describes itself as independent, saying it is not authorised by Brand or his lawyers. Who posted it on his X account has not been established.

The campaign wants Brand’s legal rights upheld while he weighs challenging his convictions, with independent scrutiny of any grounds of appeal. Its organisers acknowledge the seriousness of the offences and reject harassment or interference with witnesses.

Brand, 51, was sentenced at Southwark Crown Court on Monday, November 9, after being found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women, for offences between 2004 and 2009. He denied the allegations, and after a four-week trial he was cleared of two further rape charges and one of attempted indecent assault.

Mr Justice Joel Bennathan described him as a sexual predator and criticised his “staggering arrogance”. Brand faces lifelong sex offender notification requirements and is reported to be held at HMP Wandsworth.

Thousands signed the Change.org page within hours of the Thursday post. Critics cited by GB News questioned the timing and said attention should remain with the women who were abused.

Meta reportedly removed Brand’s Instagram and Facebook accounts permanently after the verdicts, while his X profile stayed active. A later post there accused Meta of trying to erase him and thanked X owner Elon Musk; its author has not been publicly confirmed.

That continued activity has fuelled online speculation that someone close to Brand has access, and Gallacher’s name has surfaced because of her public support for him. Speculation is not evidence: neither the organisers nor the account have named her, and the GB News report, which said it contacted the organisers and Brand’s representatives, contained no confirmation of her involvement.

Gallacher, 39, attended court with her husband during the trial and at sentencing, and the couple have three children. She has not publicly acknowledged any part in the petition.

Some supporters still dispute the outcome. Musk, a prominent defender, shared a graphic questioning the convictions before sentencing, though his comments do not change their legal status.

An online petition, however many signatures it gathers, does not create grounds for an appeal, which in England and Wales generally requires the Court of Appeal to find a conviction unsafe.

Because prisoners face restrictions on phone and internet use, a post on Brand’s account does not mean he wrote it. There is currently no verified evidence connecting Gallacher to the petition, and her support during the trial does not establish any involvement.

No successful appeal or change to Brand’s sentence has been reported, and the identity of the person who shared the petition with his millions of followers remains unknown.
