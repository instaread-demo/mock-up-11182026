6.9% Of ChatGPT Conversations Showed Purchase Intent In U.S. Panel

  • Only a small slice of chats in a U.S. opt-in ChatGPT panel involved buying.
  • SparkToro's Rand Fishkin credits Google's AI features, rather than ChatGPT, for why AI visibility matters.
  • Teams planning ChatGPT ads or visibility work get a panel-based benchmark.

A U.S. panel shows that purchase intent is rare in ChatGPT chats and that most answers mention no brand at all, a data point for teams deciding on ChatGPT visibility and ad spend.

Brands have some insight into how often purchase intent shows up in chat with ChatGPT, based on its presence in 6.9% of conversations in a U.S. opt-in panel analyzed by SparkToro.

Data was provided by Verso, which tracks users’ ChatGPT conversation history when they link their account. The sample size is 41,961 conversations, generated by 12,321 people between Jan. 1 and Oct. 29, 2026. Most of the figures are from a sub-sample which had been statistically weighted to match the demographic characteristics of the U.S. population.

In that weighted group, 87.9% of ChatGPT’s answers named no brand, product or business, which led SparkToro CEO Rand Fishkin to argue that Google’s AI Overviews and AI Mode, not ChatGPT, are what make AI visibility count.

How Often Conversations Showed Purchase Intent

The weighted data sorted chats three ways: 4.9% came from people still weighing options before buying, 2.1% asked about buying a specific brand, product or service, and 93.1% had no buying goal.

Fishkin saw this as bad news for ChatGPT’s ad prospects. He compared it with SparkToro’s December 2024 analysis of about 332 million U.S. Google searches (January 2023 to September 2024), where 15% or more fit his definition and commercial queries made up 14.5% (transactional 0.69%), though those categories don’t map onto the ChatGPT ones.

How Often Brands Came Up

Most prompts (86.7%) sought no brand information, and 12.1% of answers named at least one brand. The report’s takeaways put 12% of usage in the buyer journey, above its chart’s 6.9% purchase-intent share.

What People Used ChatGPT For

Image and design requests led with 17.6% of chats, ahead of information and entertainment (14.4%) and writing (12.7%), while shopping, brands and local services took 5.3%, with one main-goal category per chat.

How The Panel Compares With OpenAI’s Data

Compared with OpenAI’s September Signals data, the panel leaned toward images and how-to questions, while OpenAI’s mix favored writing and tech support. OpenAI says its figures cover consumer ChatGPT plans only, with no API use, and reflect global rather than U.S. shares.

About The Data

Verso, a free ChatGPT history tool, keeps imported chats for de-identified datasets it licenses, and co-founder Aaron Aknin supplied them. The panel skewed younger, female and lower-income, so 9,675 members were weighted to the 2024 Census, and the 131 heaviest users were dropped. Mostly mobile capture may undercount desktop and work use. Claude coded the chats, with a second coder agreeing on 90% of categories. SparkToro sells audience research software.

Why This Matters

Purchase-related chats came in under 7%, and Fishkin doubts ChatGPT visibility tracking is worthwhile without Bing rankings too. PPC teams should treat the 6.9% as a snapshot of a light-use consumer panel, not a ChatGPT-wide count.

Looking Ahead

Until panels capture desktop, work and API use, check figures like this against your own ChatGPT referral and ad data before budgeting. Fishkin plans further work with Verso.
