From Antalya to Shenzhen: China promotes greener cooperation

The 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting, scheduled for November 18-19, will bring together the 21 member economies. Six days earlier, an initiative was launched for high-quality carbon credit cooperation on the sidelines of COP31 in Antalya. Though the two forums have distinct mandates, they face a common challenge: how to translate climate pledges into investment that advances the sustainable development of the Global South. China's engagement in both processes creates an opportunity to align regional economic integration with pragmatic climate action.

Turning reductions into investment

The authors note that a credit usually equals one tonne of CO2-equivalent cut or removed under a program's rules, a verified result rather than a compliance allowance, and its sale can fund the work behind it.

Trust gives the market value

Since avoided emissions cannot be physically checked, they say measurement, independent checks and anti-reuse records set credible projects apart, citing the Integrity Council's Core Carbon Principles, which are separate from Paris Agreement transfer rules. Cooperation, they add, could lower verification costs without diluting quality.

Responsible buyers sustain demand

The Antalya initiative asks companies to use credits alongside their own cuts and disclose what they bought; for firms in Shenzhen, the authors frame credible claims and steady demand as an investment question.

Widening opportunities for the Global South

Many developing countries lack verifiers and monitoring capacity, and certification costs deter small projects. The initiative stresses capacity building and data sharing, a theme Vice President Han Zheng echoed at a South-South forum in Antalya on November 11.

The authors argue these countries should also help shape rules, with communities sharing fairly in proceeds.

Connecting the Shenzhen and Antalya agendas

China's Ministry of Commerce called the green economy a key APEC trade priority at a November 16 briefing in Shenzhen, while the APEC Secretariat pointed to digital infrastructure financing and trade-system interoperability.

The authors see digital records aiding traceability, link the 2026 "China Year" theme to climate partnerships, and caution that verification lowers but cannot remove project risk.

From cooperation to shared growth

They conclude that quality credits are one tool among many, and that stronger institutions can let more developing economies take part and benefit.
